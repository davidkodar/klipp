# What you can do in Klipp with an assistant

This is for answering the person, not for planning an edit. When they
ask what they can do, what you can do in Klipp, or how to get started,
answer from here, in their words, and short:

- One sentence first: you can work in Klipp with them, editing,
  grading, sound, titles, checking and exporting, by doing it in the
  open project. They see every change, and each one is a single undo
  step.
- Then the groups below that fit, one line each with an example of what
  they could ask. Five or six groups, not every line of this page, and
  no tool names.
- Then one step that fits what is open, read live with `klipp_status`:
  an empty project wants footage imported; footage with no timeline
  wants a first cut; a cut wants a review pass, a grade or an export.
- Say what needs a download the first time (some AI models), and ask
  before starting one.
- End by saying there is more, naming two or three areas you left out
  that might suit them (framing and motion, effects and layouts,
  graphics for screen recordings, syncing sound, planning an edit
  together), and that they can ask about any of them. The short answer
  is a start, not the whole list.

## Cutting and assembling

- Put footage on the timeline, trim, split, move, ripple and remove
  clips, with each clip's sound kept in step.
  *"Make a two-minute cut of this interview, keeping the best answers."*
- Cut a long take down to its usable moments, dropping the ums and the
  false starts; cut on pauses and sentence ends, never through a word.
  *"Take out the ums and long pauses."*
- Cut to music: on the beat, at the length the music allows.
  *"Make a 30-second montage of the trip to this track, cutting on the
  beat."*
- J and L cuts, speed changes (reverse too), transitions where the
  piece wants them, and multicam: sync two or more angles and switch
  between them.

## Understanding the footage

- Transcribe speech and find where something is said.
  *"Where does she talk about the budget?"*
- Find shots by what is in them: wide or close, people, motion, length.
  *"Find the close-ups of the guitar."*
- Contact sheets and frames from any moment, so we can look together.
- For screen recordings: read the text on screen, find where each step
  happens, and hide anything sensitive such as emails or keys; zoom to
  where the action is, and circle, box or spotlight what to look at.
  *"Zoom in on each click and box the Save button."*

## Framing and motion

- Reframe for vertical or square, keeping the subject in frame.
  *"Make a vertical version that keeps her face in shot."*
- Slow push-ins, pull-outs and pans across a still shot, or a frame
  that follows a subject or the action on screen.
  *"Add a slow zoom on the interview so it doesn't sit still."*
- Speed ramps, slow motion and reverse.
- Steady a shaky handheld shot.

## Effects and layouts

- Picture beside picture, a picture shrunk into a corner, split
  screens, and moves between layouts.
  *"Put the webcam in the corner over the screen recording."*
- Blur, glow, grain, sharpen and vignette.
- Backgrounds, colour cards, bars and countdowns made in the app.

## Colour

- Balance and match shots, so a scene looks like one place and time.
  *"Make these shots match the first one."*
- Creative looks from a built-in library, or the look of a reference
  file or shot. *"Give it a warm film look."*
- Skin tones, skies and single colours corrected on their own; tracked
  windows that follow a face.
- One grade over a whole scene or the whole timeline, changed once for
  every clip in it.
- Checks a colourist would make: levels, clipping, skin, consistency.

## Sound

- Clean up dialogue: wind, hum, hiss, room noise, harsh S's, and AI
  voice isolation. *"The interview has traffic noise — clean it up."*
- Levels and fades, and music that dips under speech.
- Sync a separate recorder's sound to the camera's.
  *"Sync the lav mic to the camera clips."*
- Find royalty-free music and sound effects, with their licence shown.
  *"Find calm piano music we can use commercially."*
- Narration from a script, in a choice of voices, made on this
  computer; sound effects on cuts and reveals.

## Titles and captions

- Titles, lower thirds with names and roles, animated text and cards.
  *"Add her name and title when she first speaks."*
- Captions from the speech, which you can read and correct; subtitles
  exported as a file.

## Checking and delivering

- A review pass the way an editor watches it: black or flash frames,
  jump cuts, titles too short to read, sound cut mid-word, music too
  loud under speech, and the piece held to its brief.
  *"Check the cut before I export it."*
- Export for where it is going: size, frame rate, loudness for the
  platform, captions burnt in or as a file.
  *"Export it for Instagram, vertical, with captions."*

## The project

- New projects, opening and saving, importing files, and undo for
  anything done.
- A brief for the piece — its length, platform and the people in it —
  which every check and export then follows.
- An edit plan to read and approve before anything changes, for a
  bigger job. *"Plan a 90-second cut and show me before you do it."*
- Markers, tags and picked selects to organise the footage.
- Screen recordings made from here, for tutorials and demos.
